Discovering the islands of the Gulf of Naples

I owe this trip to my friend Cecilia. We were neighbours in London; her mother is Roman, her father was born on Ischia, and the island has always been her real home. Whenever we can, we meet at her family’s house there.

Ischia, with an ischitana

Seeing a place through the eyes of someone who grew up there changes everything. Cecilia took me to her family’s favourite restaurant, and to the shoemaker in Lacco Ameno who sews custom-made sandals while you wait. We walked up the volcano among ancient woods, and spent long afternoons in the thermal baths and pools scattered around the island, some of them right by the sea.

Procida, the smaller sister

Procida is on the way from Naples to Ischia, and you cannot miss it. Swim off its coast, then walk to La Corricella, the little harbour of pastel houses you may recognise from The Postman and The Talented Mr. Ripley. Sit down at one of the restaurants on the port for spaghetti with clams.

The fishermen mending their nets on the pier are always happy to chat. In 2022 Procida was Italian Capital of Culture, and it has kept its quiet charm.

When to go

My favourite time is late April and May, when the islands are covered in wildflowers and the air smells of zagara, the lemon blossom. September and October are lovely too. Stay at least a week, so you can also spend a day in Naples.
