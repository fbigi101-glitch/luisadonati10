My dear Florence

Ask a Florentine where they are from and you will rarely hear “Italy” or “Tuscany”. “We are not Italians, we are not from Tuscany, we are from Florence.” The bond between Florentines and their city is unlike anything else I know.

I was born and raised in Rome and moved to Florence when I was fourteen. After all these years I still hesitate to call myself a Florentine in front of the locals – they can always hear my accent. And yet my surname is a very Florentine one: the Donati were one of the city’s great families, until they left Florence in 1301.

A city that keeps its soul

Tourism has made the centre crowded, but Florence has kept its character. Step a few streets away from the main sights and you find neighbourhoods where life goes on as it always has: workshops, markets, people chatting at the bar.

Florence with me

This is the Florence I love to share. On my walks we visit artisans in their workshops, sit down to a meal in a private home, discover small museums off the beaten track and wander through the local markets. I am also happy to suggest bars, cafés, restaurants, concerts and places to stay, so your days feel like those of a temporary inhabitant.
