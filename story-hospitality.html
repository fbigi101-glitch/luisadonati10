Hospitality is – first of all – a pleasure

In my family, hospitality has never been just good manners: it is a value. It is what led us to turn our Tuscan farm into a holiday resort and to restore, villa after villa, an almost abandoned hamlet – where some of my brothers and sisters still live today.

Dolce vita

Italian life revolves around good food, good wine and celebrating with friends. I still remember toasting with my brothers Damiano and Massimo to our very first bottle of Montestigliano wine.

Il dolce far niente

The sweetness of doing nothing: unhurried time, simply enjoying the day. At Montestigliano we turned an old henhouse into the Garden of the Senses, a quiet corner behind a wooden door where friends gathered for aperitivo.

Italianity is in the detail

You see it everywhere in Italy: a beautifully set table, a handwritten menu, fresh flowers. When I welcomed guests in Le Marche, I arranged the flowers and green sprigs in the rooms myself, with lavender from the garden and scented linen. A proverb from Le Marche reminds us how precious good rest is, and we have always cared about comfortable beds.

As if I were a guest

I like to offer guests the comforts I enjoy at home: organic soaps from small producers in Tuscany and Le Marche, fully equipped kitchens, towels hand-printed by an old printing house. Because hospitality is, first of all, a pleasure – for the guest and for the host.
